eightytwenty

A free, read-only connector that gives your AI assistant a slide-design playbook for case competition decks, plus search over a curated library of slide layouts.

Each library entry is an abstract layout record (region boxes, colour and type roles, hierarchy, a short note on why the slide works) and a wireframe drawn from that record. There are no original slides, names or numbers in it. Your assistant uses a reference as inspiration and builds the slide with its own tools.

No account, no sign-in. It works with assistants that connect to remote MCP servers, such as claude.ai, ChatGPT, Claude Code and Cursor; web pages cannot call it directly.

Connect

Server URL (Streamable HTTP, no authentication):

https://eightytwenty.socratic.workers.dev/mcp

claude.ai

Customize → Connectors → + Add → Add custom connector. Enter a name and the URL above, choose No sign in, and add it. In a chat, turn it on under + → Connectors, then pick its start prompt there or just ask for help with your deck.

Claude Code not yet tested

claude mcp add --transport http eightytwenty https://eightytwenty.socratic.workers.dev/mcp

Then run /eightytwenty:start, or just ask for help with your deck.

Other MCP clients not yet tested

Tools

All five are read-only. There is no way to upload to the library or list all of it.

get_playbook(stage?)
The methodology: workflow, narrative and impact slides, slide archetypes, anatomy and taglines, distinctive design, visual rules and design options, writing, a QA checklist, and how to audit the style of a deck you attach in chat. With no stage it returns the index and the workflow. Your assistant should call it before planning a deck.
find_references(purpose, slide_type?, industry?, data_type?, impact?, k?)
Searches the library by what a slide must do, with optional filters; impact (none, build, spotlight, shade_ribbon, repeat) finds slides that reference decks repeat for effect, and few exist. Returns up to eight short summaries (k, default 8), at most one per deck: id, slide type, a score, purpose, why it works, the layout's component kinds and its impact device if any, plus the library version. When planning a whole deck, use it after the slide-by-slide storyline is confirmed.
find_deck_flows(purpose, industry?, length?)
Searches the library's deck flows (how strong decks order their sections) by what a whole deck must achieve, optionally by industry and length (short, medium, long). Returns up to three summaries: deck id, arc, length, industry, section sequence, why the flow works, and a score. Use it after the slide-by-slide storyline is confirmed.
get_reference(id)
One slide: its labels (purpose, why it works, archetype, insight device, impact), its component layout, its design-DNA record (the 40 largest regions), and its wireframe image where the client shows images. Given a deck id, it returns that deck's flow: its steps in order, each with a slide id, its impact device, and whether that slide is in the library.
find_images(kind, query, k?)
Sources a logo, icon or photo for a slide being built: public-domain logo SVGs from Wikimedia Commons and Simple Icons, permissive icon SVGs from Iconify, and CC0, public-domain and CC BY photos from Openverse. Returns up to four results (k, default 3, or 4 for icons) with their licence and either the SVG text or the photo's download and page links, plus a credit line only when the licence requires one; photos come with thumbnails. The search terms are sent to those three services; see Privacy.

Try asking

  • “Use eightytwenty to plan the executive summary slide for my case on a regional grocery chain.”
  • “Find reference layouts for a slide that compares three market-entry options, and build one in my style.”
  • “Here is my deck. Audit its style with the eightytwenty playbook and fix slide 4.”

Limits

Each client IP address can make 20 tool calls a minute. Going over starts a cooldown of 1 minute that doubles for repeat bursts, up to an hour. Requests from claude.ai share one larger allowance, and searches across all users share a daily budget that resets at 00:00 UTC. Photo searches have their own daily budget; when it runs out, logos and icons still work.
