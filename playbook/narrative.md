# Narrative, chain of proof and impact slides

A deck wins on its argument before its design. Judges follow one line of reasoning from the problem to the ask; every
slide is a step in that line. Plan the storyline in text first (`workflow`, deck mode), then build slides that carry it.

## Narrative questions (deck-mode intake)

Send these as one numbered list, each with the assumption you will use if the user does not answer (from the case
prompt where possible). Skip any the prompt already answers.

1. **The end belief.** What must the judges believe at the end, in one sentence? This is the recommendation plus why it
   wins.
2. **The target market.** Who exactly is the client serving, and what do they specifically need that they are not
   getting today?
3. **The alternatives.** Which options will the judges think of themselves (do nothing, the obvious competitor move,
   the cheaper option, the prompt's own suggestion)? Why does each fail for this market?
4. **The hook.** What tension opens the deck: a number, a contradiction, a customer moment, a cost of inaction?
5. **The reasoning chain.** Which 3 to 5 steps lead from the problem to the recommendation (e.g. the market is shifting
   -> the client is losing this segment -> the segment needs X -> only option C delivers X within the constraints)?
6. **The handoffs.** What does each section hand to the next? If a section hands nothing on, it is filler or appendix.

## The storyline (text, before any slide)

One row per slide, in order:

| # | Section | Claim tagline | What it proves | Builds on | Evidence needed | Visual idea |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | Analysis | Late-shift workers are the fastest-growing segment and the least served | The target market exists and has an unmet need | 3 (market is shifting) | Segment growth, satisfaction gap | Highlight-the-one bar chart |
| 5 | Analysis | Discounts and a new app both fail late-shift workers | The obvious alternatives do not fix the need | 4 | Usage data by shift, cost of discounting | Option rows with verdict marks; shade + ribbon after it |

"Visual idea" names the composition and, where used, the impact device. Mark open evidence `[need]` and assumed facts
`[assumed]`. Read the taglines column top to bottom before sending: it must read as the whole argument.

## Chain of proof

- **Nothing before its justification.** No slide uses a fact, segment, option or number that an earlier slide has not
  established (or that the case prompt does not state).
- **Earn the recommendation.** Every recommendation is preceded by slides proving the need, the target market and the
  failure of the alternatives for that market. A recommendation that arrives before its alternatives are dismissed reads
  as the team's preference, not the answer.
- **No orphan analysis.** Every analysis slide sets up a later decision. Name that decision in the "Builds on" column of
  the slide that uses it; if no later slide builds on an analysis slide, cut it or move it to the appendix.
- **Tagline story test.** The taglines read in order tell the whole argument with no gaps and no repeats.
- **Numbers carry forward.** A number introduced in the analysis is the same number when the recommendation or the
  financials reuse it.

## Linking devices

Make the chain visible so judges never wonder why a slide is there.

- **Transition taglines** that pick up the last slide's claim ("With discounts ruled out, the gap is convenience, not
  price").
- **Recap strips**: a thin band restating the 2 to 3 findings the next section builds on.
- **Trackers**: the section tracker, plus numbered circles or icons that tie each recommendation back to the need it
  answers.
- **Callbacks** to the persona or the opening problem: the same persona, number or customer moment reappears where the
  recommendation solves it.

## Impact slides

Impact slides repeat or extend the slide just shown to land one point. They are narrative devices, not decoration.

| Device | How | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Build** | The same slide shown 2 to 4 times, revealing one element per step (a chart's bars, the steps of a process, the options one by one). | Walking through a sequence or a reveal. |
| **Spotlight** | The previous slide repeated with one element at full colour and everything else faded (white overlay ~70%, often a dashed box round the focus). | Opening each recommendation pillar from the exec summary; pointing from the issue tree to the root cause a pillar fixes; revealing a chart's key region. |
| **Shade + ribbon** | The previous slide repeated, shaded dark at ~70%, with a ribbon across it carrying the one-line so-what. The ribbon is a claim ("Neither alternative reaches late-shift workers"), never a topic ("Key takeaway"). | Landing the key insight or a turning point. |
| **Repeat / recap** | An earlier slide (exec summary, issue tree, recommendation overview) shown again, often spotlighted on the current part. | Re-orienting before a new section or before the ask. |

**When**: at turning points only. Landing the key insight; the problem -> solution hinge; just before the
recommendation; a recap before the ask; the transition into each recommendation pillar.

**How**: always built on the slide just shown (or the earlier slide being recalled), duplicated so layout, tracker and
footer are identical; only the overlay, highlight or revealed element changes. Name it `<parent> - <device> <element>`
(e.g. `Executive Summary - spotlight Pillar 2`). The ribbon and spotlight copy follows the tagline rules (`writing`).

**Sparingly**: about one per section. Three in a row drags in a 10-minute presentation. Reference deck flows mark their
impact slides; those counts show where strong decks used them, not how many you need.
