# Make it distinctive

Runs on every slide, unprompted. Left alone, a slide gets the most typical layout for its job, because that is what the
archetype table describes. Every competing team draws from the same pool, so the result is competent and forgettable.
Users get their best results by asking "make this more unique" afterwards; this chapter does that step up front.

## The five steps

**1. Generate three concepts before building anything.** Silently sketch:
- **A, baseline**: the archetype (`archetypes`).
- **B, from the client's world**: borrow the structure of an object, artifact or process native to this client or
  industry, and make the content live inside it. An airline's recommendations as a boarding pass with gates and rows; a
  gifting slide as a gift tag or checkout cart; a hotel portfolio as a floor plan with rooms as properties; a bank's
  customer journey as a statement with line items; a rideshare roadmap as a trip route with stops; a food company's
  options as a menu with prices = cost.
- **C, from the shape of the argument or data**: let the logic dictate the composition, using the references from
  `find_references` and the composition library below. A dominant hero number with evidence orbiting it; a see-saw for
  a trade-off; a waterfall for a value build; a ladder to goal for staged growth; a before/after mirror; a pyramid for a
  hierarchy; a Mekko for share by segment; a butterfly chart for two segments compared; a 100-people pictogram for "7 in
  10 customers"; a winding road map with pins for a phased rollout; one annotated real artifact (the client's app
  screen, store photo or receipt) with numbered callouts.

**2. Pick B or C unless both fail the clarity test.** Clarity test: a judge seeing the slide for 5 seconds can state the
claim. Fall back to A only if neither passes, and say so in one line. **A concept may reshape the archetype's required
parts, never drop them**: an executive summary drawn as a boarding pass still shows Problem, Recommendation and Outcome.

**3. Give every slide one signature element.** Something only this slide in this deck has: the client-world metaphor, a
hero number at 60pt+, a real artifact with callouts, a recurring persona in context, an unusual chart form chosen for
the data. Two signature elements compete; zero is generic. The signature element and the insight device are often the
same thing; that is ideal.

**4. Run the swap test.** Mentally replace the client's name and logo with a competitor's. If the slide still works
unchanged, it is generic. Tie the layout, imagery, labels or example to something specific to this client, this case
prompt or this persona.

**5. Vary across the deck.** Read the neighbouring slides first and note which concepts and insight devices the deck
already uses. Do not reuse a composition within a section, and avoid three consecutive slides with the same grid. Do
carry recurring motifs (the persona, the client-world metaphor, a consistent marker for "our recommendation") so the
deck reads as one story, not a sampler.

**Guardrails (distinctive is not decorative):**
- Fonts, colours, header block, tracker and footer stay consistent. They change only deck-wide through an approved
  design mock, never slide by slide. Distinctiveness lives in the body's structure.
- Every shape carries meaning. No clip art, gradients, drop shadows, 3D effects, or stock imagery behind text.
- The metaphor must hold up when explained aloud in one sentence. If it needs a legend, it is too clever; drop to C or A.
- Numbers stay readable and comparable; never distort a chart for style.
- Never edit the slide master or existing layouts in place without confirmation. If the composition needs a reusable
  layout, add a new one named for its purpose.

## Composition library

Clean, white-background structures in grey plus one brand colour, in the style of think-cell chart templates. Use them
for concept C, restyled into the deck's fonts and colours and built natively (native charts plus shapes for
annotations).

| Slide job | Structures worth borrowing |
|---|---|
| Market sizing / share | Mekko (% or unit axis) for share by segment and size; stacked 100% bar for mix; bar of pie to break out one segment; 100-people pictogram with a big % for adoption. |
| Growth / trends | Stacked column or line with **CAGR arrows**, **total difference arrows** (+18%) and **level difference brackets**; area 100% for mix shift; column + line combination for volume vs margin. |
| Competitor / benchmark | Clustered column or bar with "our brand" in brand colour and competitors in grey; butterfly / tornado for two segments compared criterion by criterion; scatter or bubble for positioning; football field for valuation ranges. |
| Problem analysis | Problem tree and decision tree; cause-and-effect (causes left, chevron, effects right, conclusion column in brand colour); iceberg or two-obstacles layouts. |
| Options / prioritisation | 2x2 and 2x3 prioritisation matrices; nine-block matrix; traffic-light or good/neutral/bad dot matrix; pros and cons halves; four-block growth-share matrix; aspect matrix with a plotted winner. |
| Customer | Journey with touchpoints on an emotional curve and a "voice of customer" row; journey dashboard; target-market hub with segments radiating; Venn for an underserved overlap. |
| Strategy / recommendation | Pyramid (3 to 6 layers, apex highlighted); ladder to goal; three core elements (concentric circles); building blocks or pillars (roof = vision, pillars = initiatives); hub-and-spoke for an ecosystem; value chain chevrons with the changed step highlighted; business model canvas. |
| Process / implementation | Numbered process with one step highlighted; process with a results row; swim lanes for partner roles; Gantt with lead and status columns, milestone diamonds and a shaded "now" band; milestone flags or a winding road map with pins; arched timeline with the target year bold. |
| Financials | Build-up and build-down **waterfalls** for revenue-to-profit or cost bridges; funnel waterfall for conversion economics; stacked column with yearly totals; sensitivity table. |
| KPIs / dashboard | Dashboard and traffic-light tiles; gauges against target; ring diagrams comparing %; goal icons. |
| Geography | Country or region maps with pins, shaded regions and a stats sidebar. |
| Quotes / voice | One oversized italic quote with attribution; six key messages grid. |

Conventions:
- **Grey plus one highlight**: every series grey except the one that carries the argument.
- **Annotations on the chart**, not in a separate box: CAGR arrows, difference arrows, value or target lines, baseline
  breaks for one outsized bar.
- **Comments sidebar**: chart ~70% width; a right column headed with a mini-claim holds 2 to 3 bullets.
- Chart titles with units in brackets (`Revenue [USD m]`), legends at the bottom, minimal gridlines.

Avoid: 3D cubes and pyramids, sunburst and spiderweb charts (hard to read in a room), agenda, schedule, team-card and
calendar slides, and template text density (templates show layout, not how much text to use).

## Key insight devices (rotate, don't repeat)

A so-what box at the bottom of every slide becomes wallpaper by slide 5; judges stop reading it and it usually restates
the tagline. The insight is more findable when it sits **on the evidence** and when its form changes. The tagline states
the claim; the device points at the proof. Never repeat the tagline's words in the device.

| Device | What it looks like | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| 1. On-chart annotation | CAGR arrow, difference arrow (`+18%`), bracket, target line (`Goal: 40%`), baseline break, in brand colour on the chart. | Time series, comparisons. |
| 2. Highlight the one | Everything grey, one bar / segment / step / row / region in brand colour with a direct label ("Our target segment: 35%"). | Charts, processes, matrices, maps, funnels. |
| 3. Hero number | One 60pt+ number with a one-line caption, about a third of the slide; evidence smaller around it. | Proof that is a single stat. |
| 4. Comments sidebar | Chart ~70% width; right column with a bold mini-claim and 2 to 3 bullets. | Dense charts that need nuance. |
| 5. Arrival point | The layout ends at the insight: last process step, pyramid apex, top rung, final road-map pin, conclusion column after a cause-and-effect chevron, in brand colour. | Processes, roadmaps, hierarchies. |
| 6. Hub | The insight in the centre of a hub-and-spoke, target-market or Venn layout; spokes are the evidence. | Ecosystems, overlaps, partner models. |
| 7. Numbered callouts | Brand-colour numbered circles on the chart, map or mockup, each linked to a short note beside it. | Mockups, maps, annotated artifacts, busy charts. |
| 8. Pull quote | One oversized italic quote, key phrase bold, with attribution. | Customer or expert voice as proof. |
| 9. Verdict marks | Checks vs crosses, traffic lights, Harvey ball totals, winner row or column in brand colour. | Options, pros and cons, scoring. |
| 10. Spotlight | Fade everything but one element (`narrative`), on its own slide or within the slide. | Transitions, reveals. |
| 11. Tagline only | No extra element; the exhibit is self-evident. | Clean exhibits; gives the eye a rest. |
| 12. So-what bar | Full-width bar or dashed box at the bottom. | Only where several panels must be tied together, e.g. a section-closing synthesis. |

**Rotation rules:**
- Read the neighbours first. Never the same device on two adjacent slides.
- No device on more than about a third of the deck's content slides.
- So-what bar on at most 1 in 5 content slides, never twice in a row.
- Name the device shape `Insight_<device>` (`Insight_CAGRArrow`, `Insight_HeroNumber`, `Insight_SoWhatBar`) so the
  deck's mix can be counted on later runs.

**Findability test:** a judge's eye lands on the insight within 2 seconds. That needs contrast: the insight is the only
brand-colour emphasis in the body (besides header bars), or clearly the largest type, or the only thing not greyed. If
two elements compete, demote one.
